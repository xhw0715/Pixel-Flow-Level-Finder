What Is Meowdoku?
Learn the rules in a minute, then reason your way through the night

Meowdoku is a cat-themed logic puzzle that combines Sudoku constraints with Minesweeper-style deduction. The board is divided into colored regions. Place exactly one cat in each region while keeping every cat away from the others. The rules take less than a minute; one wrong mark can quietly break the whole board.

Core rules: three lines are the whole game

Remember these three rules and you already know how to play. The rest is deduction.

1

One cat per colored region

Each colored region contains exactly one cat, never more and never fewer.

2

One cat per row and column

As in Sudoku, no row or column may contain more than one cat.

3

Cats cannot touch

Two cats cannot be adjacent horizontally, vertically, or diagonally. This is the easiest rule to forget.

Four steps for a smooth solve

1

Take the free placements

If a region has only one available cell, or a row has only one open space, that position is forced. Place those cats first.

2

Mark exclusions immediately

After placing a cat, eliminate its row, column, region, and all eight neighboring cells. The board becomes clearer as you go.

3

Use region locking

If every available cell in a region is on the same row, that row's cat must come from that region. Eliminate the rest of the row.

4

Never guess

You have three lives and no undo. When no placement is forced, inspect the most constrained region instead of tapping at random.

Features and game mechanics

Light, offline, and low-pressure, with a difficulty ceiling that keeps rising.

🐾 Region logic, not number filling

There are no digits, only colors and spatial relationships. Elimination leads to the unique solution, making it ideal for pure deductive players.

❤️ Three lives, no countdown

A wrong cat costs a heart, but there is no time pressure. Think through each placement before committing.

Expert tips for a stuck board

Most constrained region first

Do not stare at the largest region. Start with the one that has the fewest available cells; two candidates often lock its row or column.

Read two or three rows together

If a small set of rows can only be supplied by the same few regions, those regions are reserved. Eliminate the remaining cells in those rows.

A wrong X costs nothing

When unsure, mark an exclusion instead of placing a cat. A wrong X wastes a second; a wrong cat costs a life.

Recheck your exclusions

Most dead boards are not missing a new idea. An earlier X removed the cell the solution needed. Audit the marks before searching for a new inference.

Frequently asked questions

How do you play Meowdoku?

The board is split into colored regions. Place one cat in each region, one per row and column, and keep cats from touching horizontally, vertically, or diagonally. Double-tap a cell to place a cat.

Is Meowdoku free?

Yes. It is free on Google Play and the App Store, supported by ads and optional purchases. You can finish every level without paying.

How many levels are there?

The game includes more than 15,000 levels, moving from small grids to genuinely difficult boards. The guide currently has 1,370 video walkthroughs and keeps adding more.

Where can I find a level answer?

Search the Meowdoku Guide level library by level number. Each page includes a video walkthrough and a written explanation.

Why do I keep getting stuck on the same level?

Most stalls come from an earlier exclusion that removed a needed cell, not from a lack of inspiration. Recheck every X, then look for a region whose candidates all share one row or column.

Does Meowdoku work offline?

Yes. After installation, normal play works without an internet connection.
